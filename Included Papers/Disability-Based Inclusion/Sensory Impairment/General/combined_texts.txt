Abstract . The government has established special schools to cater to the needs of children with disabilities but they are often segregated rather than receiving equitable opportunities. Artificial Intelligence has opened new ways to promote special education with advanced learning tools. These tools enable to adapt to a typical classroom set up for all the students with or without disabilities. To ensure social equity and the same classroom experience, a coherent solution is envisioned for inclusive education. This paper aims to propose a cost-effective and integrated Smart Learning Assistance (SLA) tool for Inclusive Education using Deep Learning and Computer Vision techniques. It comprises speech to text and sign language conversion for hearing impaired students, sign language to text conversion for speech impaired students, and Braille to text for communicating with visually impaired students. The tool assists differently-abled students to make use of various teaching-learning opportunities conferred to them and ensures convenient two-way communication with the instructor and peers in the classroom thus makes learning easier.

Keywords: Inclusive classroom, image processing, computer vision, deep learning, artificial intelligence

## 1. Introduction

The education system plays a very crucial role in the overall growth and development of a country. India, one of the fastest developing countries globally, took cognizance of the situation and underwent many reforms in strengthening its education system. It is believed that education is not a privilege but a right of everyone. The students with disabilities may wish to contribute to the development of the country, but they generally do not get equitable opportunities. An analytical report published by enabled.in mentioned that 2.21% (2.68 Cr) of the total population (121 crores) has disabilities, out of which 19%

The Indian government has implemented several policies for the last two decades concerning educational reforms for the disabled. One of these policies is 'Sarva Shiksha Abhiyan' (SSA), aiming at free education for disabled children of 6-14 years of age. Free inclusive education is an integral part of SSA to provide ' education to all '. Inclusive education is a learning environment where children with and without disabilities study in the same classroom [2]. The purpose of inclusive education is to make classrooms impartial to all students regardless of any disparity. Inclusive education is a birthright for every child and not a privilege. The Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA) clearly states that all children with disabilities should be edu- cated with non-disabled peers and have access to the general education curriculum. This act includes identifying the need for a comprehensive environment that sensitizes to the needs of children with disabilities in educational institutes. Most schools still do not practice inclusive education and are not sympathetic to the disabled [3]. A major barrier could be the unavailability and affordability of special equipment to implement inclusive classrooms. Sensitizing with the needs of the disabled will not only improve the education system but also enable them to be a more inclusive section of society where they could realize their true potential.

Several items, equipment, or product systems, known as Assistive Technologies (ATs), are available to increase, maintain, and improve the functional capabilities of individuals with disabilities. These technologies have helped children with disabilities to pursue their education. The assistive product industry is currently limited and specialized, primarily serving high-income markets. There is a lack of state funding, nationwide service delivery systems, user-centered research and development, procurement systems, quality and safety standards, and context-appropriate product design for implementing assistive products and technologies. To facilitate the teaching-learning in an inclusive classroom, an economical and consolidated Smart Learning Assistance (SLA) tool is proposed in this work with the following characteristics:

- a. This proposed tool encompasses 4 modules specific to an instructor, speech-impaired, visually-impaired, and hearing-impaired students. The procedures integrated into the tool include Sign Language to English text, speech to English text, speech to Sign language, and Braille to English text conversion to make classrooms more inclusive for students with speech, hearing, and visual impairments.

## 2. Related work

In 1974, Assistive technologies (ATs) were first invented to aid individuals with visual, speech, and hearing impairments [7]. Since its inception, it reasonably justified its cause, but it had the significant drawback of being extremely expensive. However, developments in Artificial Intelligence techniques made it possible for ATs to be implemented in less complicated devices that are moderately priced, such as Talking Calculators .

One of the very famous devices used for hearing deficit students is I-communicator [8]. It is an application developed by Interactive Solutions Inc. (ISI) for the translation of speech to sign language. It can be used as a dictionary to search for definitions [8]. Another useful device for students with hearing impairment employs boneconduction . It uses the conduction of sound to the inner ear primarily through the bones of the skull, allowing the listener to perceive audio content without blocking the ear canal [9]. It is an alternative to a regular hearing aid for those with problems in their outer or middle ears. It is useful for conductive and mixed hearing losses. Aboneconductionhearing device relies on a working cochlea , which is the hearing organ in an inner ear, to send sound to the brain [10]. These devices may provide fluent two-way communication between the student with hearing impairment and the instructor in a classroom.

For students with speech impairment, Google AI Labs has developed an algorithm capable of tracking the movement of the user's hands with the camera on a mobile device. The algorithm requires, however, a considerable amount of resources for processing data and thus has a hefty response time. To reduce the resource requirements, the position and size of the palm are taken into consideration for the detection of movement in place of the entire hand and is more distinct and regular [11]. This way, speech impaired students can easily communicate without any additional hardware.

To address speech disability, an innovative technology using Artificial Intelligence was invented by a group of students at the University of Washington, known as SignAloud [12]. It incorporates a pair of gloves that transliterate American Sign Language (ASL) into English. The gloves have sensors that track the user's hand movements and then send the data to a computer system via Bluetooth. The computer system analyses the data and matches it to English words, which are then spoken aloud by a digital voice [12]. The students can use these gloves to communicate using sign language gestures of their hands, and the instructor can interpret it by the digital voice.

As visually impaired students are comfortable using Braille to read and write, Braille translators and screen readers like SMART Brailler [13] have proved to be very beneficial for them. Braille translator is a software program that translates a script into Braille cells and sends it to a Braille embosser, which produces a hard copy in Braille script of the original text. The braille translator makes it convenient for both the instructor and the visually impaired student to communicate, as the instructor reads the message in the English language, and the student conveys their message using Braille. The SMART Brailler is an electronic device that has a video screen and provides audio feedback. It displays and speaks letters and words as a student brailles them, which provides instant feedback to a student, allowing him or her to work independently. The video screen on the device is designed to allow parents of children with visual impairments and teachers who educate such students in inclusive classrooms to observe visually in Roman letters what students are writing in braille.

The solutions mentioned above are devised to assist differently-abled people, each targeting a specific type of disability. Moreover, these solutions are not affordable to a lower stratum of society. Therefore, a cost-effective and integrated Smart Learning Assistance (SLA) tool is envisioned for students with different disabilities so that they could share the same classroom with regular students.

## 3. Research methodology

The objective of the SLA tool is to help students with various impairments to increase their engagement in the class. This tool bridges the communication gap between the instructor and disabled students so that they could seamlessly interact with their classmates as well as the instructor. The proposed tool is intended for different types of specially-abled students, viz. visual impaired, hearing impaired and speech impaired students, and the instructors. The detailed working of the tool is presented in Fig. 1.

To ensure the desired function of this tool, the instructor should have a microphone throughout the lectures to avoid background noises, and the webcam should be enabled in the computer system or digital device in which the SLA tool is installed.

TheSLAtoolisdesigned using HTML 5, CSS, and JavaScript, and has been implemented using Python 3.7.3. The Graphical User Interface (GUI) of the tool is shown in Fig. 2. The working of the tool was tested both on Windows 10 and mac OS Catalina. It supports both English and Hindi languages.

Each of the three categories of differently-abled students and the instructor has a different use of this coherent tool. Hence, separate modules or modes of operation for various disabilities are available to address their distinct needs. The tool provides 4 options/modes: Instructor module or Mode 0 , Hearing-impairment module or Mode 1 , Speechimpairment module or Mode 2 , Visual-impairment module, or Mode 3 . The tool operates by giving voice commands to the user, which increases its accessibility and responsiveness. The voice commands are primarily used for visually impaired students. The users can choose the appropriate module or mode as per their requirements. The function of each of these modules or modes is explained in the following subsections.

## 3.1.1. Speech to text conversion

In this mode, if the instructor chooses text as the output format, the audio input of the instructor is converted into the English text using the ' Speech Recognition ' API (Application Programming Interface) and PyAudio library of Python, which requires an active internet connection. It can also be used in microcontrollers such as Raspberry Pi with an external microphone.

After selecting this option, the instructor starts the lecture and his/her voice is captured by the microphone. The input audio is then recognized by the PyAudio library, which is converted into English text and is made available at the instructor's screen or device. As the instructor's screen is shared with the students, they can view the transcribed speech on their devices. This will enable the speech and hearingimpaired students to follow the instructions.

## 3.1.2. Speech to sign language conversion

The audio input of the instructor is converted into Sign Language gestures as images or GIFs (Graphics Interchange Format) if the instructor selects the output format as Sign Language gestures . Sign Language GIFs of some general conversation dialogues are included in the database. So, if the input of the teacher received by the microphone, is recognized by the PyAudio library, the tool checks the corresponding audio transcription in the database and searches for the corresponding GIF in the database. If the Sign Language GIF for that dialogue exists in the database, it is displayed in a new window using the Tkinter package. Tkinter is the standard GUI library of Python to create GUI applications. In case, the match is not found in the database, the audio input of the instructor's speech is fragmented into alphabets and converted into corresponding Sign Language gestures as shown in Fig. 3. The database contains gestures of alphabets in the form of images. These images are displayed on the screen of the instructor as well as students, in sequence, corresponding to the spelling of the words spoken by him and assists the hearing impaired students to follow the instructions.

## 3.2. Hearing-impairment module or mode 1

In general, students with impaired hearing are proficient in reading the text and if they also lack speech ability, they can use Sign Language as a mode of communication. So, whenever the instructor gives any instruction either by text message or by Sign Language gestures, the instruction is displayed on the shared screen. This mode helps students who are hard of hearing to communicate with the instructor.

## 3.3. Speech-impairment module or mode 2

Students with speech impairment can communicate through written text, but they generally prefer to communicate through Sign Language [14]. This tool ensures that the student is at convenience to use Sign Language for communication. To start the conversation, students have to click on the ' Start the conversation using sign language ' button, then the movements of their hands and other gestures are captured by the front camera of their device. These gestures are converted to readable text and enable the instructor to communicate with them. The Sign Language to text conversion is implemented using Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) model. A database of 2-D images is created for training the model and the process is described as follows.

## 3.3.1. Database creation

A directory structure is created for each category of the sign gestures for training the CNN model. Several images are taken for each 26 sign gestures of the alphabets, 10 digits, and some common phrases to achieve high accuracy for sign recognition. For example, to train the CNN model on the Sign Language gesture for the alphabet 'A', the directory contains 100 images of the same. All the sign gestures are based on the Indian Sign Language convention [15].

## 3.3.2. Image acquisition

The front camera of a device is used to capture the input in the form of Sign Language gestures from the speech impaired student. The OpenCV library is used for accessing the front camera and image processing. The best images are captured in different lighting conditions and at different angles on a plain white/light-colored background. The gestures captured as an image are adjusted at a distance to get the desired image clarity. These images are preprocessed before storing them in training and testing databases.

## 3.3.3. Image pre-processing

The image taken from the front camera of the deviceis converted to a grey-scale image, i.e. the RGB image is converted to a black and white image. Then background subtraction is performed to extract the foreground image from the background image. The black and white images, thus obtained, are used to train the CNN model and are called Image Frames .

## 3.3.4. Training the CNN model

The Sequential model is utilized to train the CNN model. The training input consisting of filtered and shrunk images originally stored in 2-D format are flattened to 1-D to pass to the dense layers . Further, the model contains three additional hidden layers to identify the latent relationship of the features from the flattened data. The actual image classification happens in the output layers , which is the final layer. The two dropout layers with a 50% and 30% probability of deactivating the hidden units respectively are added to reduce the model's over-fitting. Since this is a labeled categorical classification, the Softmax activation function is used in the final layer.

## 3.4. Visual-impairment module or mode 3

Visually impaired students are skilled in reading and writing using Braille, and with the advancement in technology, they can even type on any device using braille keyboards. The students can start the visual-impairment module through the voice commands integrated into the tool. They can enter the braille text as an input to the tool using their braille keyboards that are converted into English text for communicating with the instructor.

## 3.4.1. Braille to english text conversion

A dictionary of grade 2 Braille [16] is created to translate the Braille input into the English text. Braille signs are mapped to alphabets, digits, contractions, and punctuations. In grade 2 Braille , a cell can represent a shortened form of a word. Many cell combinations are created to represent common words, making this the most popular of the grades of Braille. There are part-word contractions , which often standin for common suffixes or prefixes, and whole-word contractions , in which a single cell represents a complete commonly used word [17].

Fig. 6. a) Training and Validation Accuracy curve b) Training and Validation Loss curve.



Students are instructed to use an escape sequence if they wish to enter any numeric input (0-9). Digits in Braille use the same symbols as it has for the first 10 letters of the alphabet (A-J). For instance, the number '8' and the letter 'h' are both represented by ' '. An escape code ( ) is placed before numbers to differentiate them from letters. Therefore '8' is actually ' ' whereas 'h' is only ' '.

The Braille input to English text translation is achieved as follows: The input sentence comprising of Braille symbols is divided into words. If a word in braille is a contraction , then it is checked against the corresponding text stored in the dictionary, and the given braille symbols of the contraction are mapped to the corresponding English text. The remaining individual words in Braille are broken down into their constituent Braille symbols and mapped to their corresponding alphabets and digits. The Braille text is converted into the English text, using a grade 2 Braille dictionary, and the final text will appear on the instructor's screen. This way, the students can convey their messages to the instructors and their classmates effectively.

A perfect Braille translation can only be done by humans, as it requires an understanding of the content at hand. Moreover, many Braille contractions follow pronunciation and programming which can be quite fiddly. For instance, the 'gh' contraction that is used in 'tough' would not be used in 'high.' Hence, the Braille Translator used in the proposed SLA tool does not include partial contractions , which may increase the complexity of the code [18]. In this way, the tool enables the instructors to understand and clarify the doubts of the students with visual impairment during the lecture.

## 5. Conclusions

Recent advancements in Artificial Intelligence technologies have transformed the way Inclusive Education is being realized nowadays. The proposed tool provides a means to bridge the communication gap which is usually felt by the students with speech, hearing, and visual impairments in a regular classroom set-up. It may serve as one of the best solutions to accomplish seamless two-way communication of disabled students with the instructor, thus eliminating the need for special classrooms for them. As the existing open-source technologies are used to design and implement the SLA tool, it is also affordable for a common student. The user acceptance testing of the tool was performed with the differently-abled students and the feedback obtained from them through discussions was utilized to improve its performance.

This paper presented an SLA tool using various python packages and libraries such as Py Audio , Speech Recognition , Tkinter , and Open CV , exploiting deep learning and computer vision techniques. To implement the Sign to the text conversion, the Sequential CNN model was trained and utilized. The validation accuracy for the trained CNN was obtained as 99.09%. Moreover, the Hearing-Impaired and the Visually-Impaired modules provided correct results almost all the time while the Speech-impaired module could recognize the Sign-language input gestures correctly 45 times out of 50.

However, for Sign language to text conversion, the CNN model was trained only on 26 alphabets, ten digits, and 4-5 phrases. The training process took few seconds, but if the tool needs to be trained on the whole English vocabulary, the training process may require substantial computational power, time, and storage, which will necessitate the use of high-end systems for the execution of the tool.

Furthermore, with the use of more advanced technologies, the functionality of the proposed tool can be extended to improve its Accuracy , Usability , Reliability , and Comfortability . The datasets on which the tool has been trained can be improvised by adding more relevant data to achieve a dedicated system for the differently-abled students. It can also serve as a strong base for developing more sophisticated and customized systems for an inclusive classroom. The SLA tool can be integrated with the devices, generally used by specially-abled students, to enhance their productivity and responsiveness.

## An Intelligent Educational and Communication Tool for Students with Visual and Auditory Impairments

I. Samu Idhayan 1 , Getzi Jeba Leelipushpam Paulraj 2 , Immanuel Johnraja Jebadurai 3

1 Computer Science and Engineering, Karunya Institute of Technology and Sciences, Coimbatore, India 2 Associate Professor, Division of CSE, Karunya Institute of Technology and Sciences, Coimbatore, India 3 Professor, Division of CSE, Karunya Institute of Technology and Sciences, Coimbatore, India E-mail:  1 isamu@karunya.edu.in,  2 getzi@karunya.edu,  3 immanueljohnraja@gmail.com

AbstractThe modern digital world presents very large communication  barriers  for  people  with  combined  visual and  auditory  impairments,  many  times  including  ineffective participation in the processes of education and social interaction. This paper introduces a mobile application aimed at  bridging  these  gaps  with  a  whole  new  Morse  code-based comprehensive  platform  that  assists  in  communication  and learning. Other features will include Morse Typing Window for Morse-based typing, Text to Morse, and Speech to Morse, which will convert the written and spoken language to Morse. It includes Morse for PDF, integrating Morse into the test files to make them more accessible. Using the Article Reader feature, the user will be able to take articles and convert them to Morse for study. An integrated Tutorial will walk one through how to learn Morse code and how to use the app. An NLP-powered word suggestion model increases the efficiency of typing and thus  makes  communication  smooth  and  quicker.  Tested  in a  school  environment,  the  application  received  very  positive feedback about its role in empowering students with multiple disabilities.  Such  a  tool  is  a  great  example  of  how  advanced information  processing  combined  with  accessible  design  can realize  the  full  potential  of  intelligent  assistive  technology  to support  inclusive  education  and  communication  of  persons with complex accessibility needs.

Keywords: Assistive Technology, Disabilities, Communication Devices,  Visual  Impairment,  Auditory  Impairment,  Morse Code,  Mobile  Applications,  Natural  Language  Processing, Accessibility, Inclusive Education

## I. Introduction

With  the  world  having  taken  up  more  of  an  upheld face  towards  the  digital  era,  communication  is  key  to most  social  interactions  and  education.  However,  people with combined visual and auditory impairments have very limited opportunities to participate in these spheres of life [1].  The  traditional  communicative  continuum  practices are  not  accessible  to  them,  which  makes  many  of  those persons isolated and unable even to express their thoughts or learn anything. This gap underlines the immediate need for creative solutions that could grant people with multiple disabilities communication and learning skills [2].

Such challenges are better addressed by Morse code, given  that  it  is  a  time-tested  communication  system.  The binary  nature  of  the  Morse  code,  comprising  short  and long  signals,  can  be  translated  into  tactile  feedback  quite effectively in order to make things accessible for users [3]. Through this approach, we are able to undertake tool design that allows users to communicate independently and access information in an inclusive manner [4].

This  paper  presents  a  mobile  application  intended  to provide  a  platform  for  visually  and  auditorily  challenged people to learn and use Morse code. The proposed system introduces  a  Morse  Typing  Window  that  allows  users  to input  Morse  code  through  tactile  feedback,  thus  enabling efficient communication with peers and educators. The Text to Morse feature involves the translation of written text into Morse code, fitting for those who make use of text methods of communication [5]. Another feature is Speech to Morse; besides, one can convert spoken words into Morse code to further expand the modes of communication. The Morse for PDF  feature  integrates  Morse  code  with  PDF  documents so  that  users  can  gain  access  to  the  educational  materials in a format compatible with their needs. This allows users to have articles read to them, and if one wants, to change those articles to Morse for studying. The app comes with an integrated Tutorial that teaches Morse code to users, as well as ways of using the application effectively. The application uses an NLP-driven word suggestion model that is capable of  predicting  and  suggesting  words  when  users  type  in Morse code. It enhances the efficiency of typing and at the same time reduces cognitive overload for the users [6]. This in turn enables smoother, and even faster, communication.

This  application  has  been  tested  in  a  school  setting, where it received positive feedback from students, thereby underlining its ability to empower the disabled. It becomes a model for how technology can serve inclusive education and  communication  for  individual  people  with  complex access challenges by effectively merging advanced information processing with accessible design. This paper attempts  to  delve  into  the  development  and  effectiveness of  this  innovative  solution.  It  also  tries  to  show  how  the innovation finds application in today's digital environment to  determine  its  potential  to  create  more  inclusion  within educational and social processes.

## II. State of the Art: History and Context

While communication is the axis around which social interactions and educational engagement revolve, those with combined visual and auditory impairments go through so many barriers that inhibit these activities. The World Health Organization estimated that currently, more than 1.5 billion people worldwide are suffering from some form or degree of hearing loss and will increase up to 2.5 billion by 2050, of whom over a quarter are said to be suffering from disabling hearing loss that affects the health and quality of life [7]. Moreover,  at  least  2.2  billion  people  in  the  world  have either a near or distance vision impairment; out of these, at least 1 billion of the cases are preventable or still remaining unattended  [8].  Of  these,  a  large  portion  are  recorded with  dual  disabilities  both  visual-auditory  impairments experiencing double disadvantages in communicating and receiving  information.  These  are  usually  associated  with isolation  and  frustration  in  educational  settings  because the  traditional  teaching  methods  do  not  accommodate diversities.

## A.  Challenges of Traditional Communication Methods

Traditional methods of communication for people with visual and auditory impairments have relied heavily on some form of communication involving tactile sensation, such as tactile  sign  language,  Braille,  and  special  communication boards. Tactile sign language requires a reliance on the sense of  touch  whereby  the  individual  feels  what  signs  another person is making. However, this then becomes a restrictive technique  since  it  entirely  relies  on  the  skills  of  the  user relating to sign language and is rather hard to learn, in which case it may become a barrier to proper communication when one is trying to pass information to a person who has not been trained.

Another traditional method of communication widely used is  Braille,  which  is  a  tactile  writing  system  used  by visually  impaired  people  to  access  information  in  written form. At the same time, it does not provide the solutions needed for communicational interaction among individuals with  both  visual  and  auditory  impairments.  Furthermore, specialized communication boards, sometimes with raised symbols or textures, utilize some sort of communication, but these often require significant adjustment and familiarity by the  communicator  and  the  one  receiving  the  information. These techniques inadequately prepare an individual with  dual  disabilities  for  educational  and  social  settings, often proving to be a hindrance in either fully expressing themselves or comprehending the expressions of others.

The  gap  in  communication  increases  in  the  case  of people  affected  with  dual  disabilities,  living  in  a  world devoid of inclusive educational environments and resources. Their  unique  needs  make  traditional  methods  of  teaching non-accommodative; hence, they always feel isolated and frustrated. On top of that, the gap in technology just makes things worse. While newer models in assistive technology have  leveled  the  playing  field  for  people  with  singular disabilities,  many  tools  remain  inadequate  or  completely inaccessible for people with impairments in double categories.

## B.  Current Technologies and Their Limitations

Recent development in assistive technologies has tried to bridge this communication gap for people suffering from

dual disabilities, specifically those having combined visual and auditory impairments. Current technologies include:

Tactile sign language  devices  provide  the  facility of  tactile  sign  language  by  converting  signs  into  either vibrations or tactile symbols. However, the effectiveness of these devices is still limited by requiring both users involved to know tactile sign language [9]. Similarly, Braille displays are electronic displays that provide better access to written information by enabling users to read text from other digital devices.  Although  these  are  of  immense  importance  to visually impaired divers, the technology stops here and does not address the auditory aspects of communication; hence this is a problem for people with auditory deficiencies [10].

Speech recognition software dictates spoken languages into text to be presented in order for visually impaired users to present information in that type of format. This does not, however, help the users with auditory deficiencies by this technology. Furthermore, it does not have support for haptic interaction.  Moreover,  speech  recognition  is  sensitive  to errors when the environment is noisy, which jeopardizes its overall efficiency [11].

On a similar note, several AR solutions aim to deliver information regarding visual content through haptic output and  other  senses.  Of  these,  none  are  personalized  or customized for people with dual disabilities. The mere fact that the messages in AR applications have something to do with vision makes it completely unreachable to those people who cannot see and hence shuts more doors to information access [12]. Furthermore, the communication boards using symbols are made up of symbols for words or sets of words. While  these  would  make  communication  possible,  they require a great deal of adaptation and familiarity on the part of  both  interlocutors.  This  level  of  reliance  on  the  users' understanding may make them unsuitable for any quick or dynamic form of communication, thus placing people with dual disabilities at a disadvantage [13].

Technologies  proposed  so  far  rarely  offer  integrated solutions  to  put  together  sight  and  hearing  impairments as  a  rule.  Without  communicative  devices  that  have  been universally designed, there is a barrier created for the full participation  of  a  person  with  impairments  in  both  vision and hearing in educational and social contexts, which calls for  new solutions that support democratic communication and interaction.

In that respect, Morse code becomes helpful for people who  cannot  see  or  hear;  it  grants  them  independence in  communication,  giving  signals  in  the  form  of  tactile feedback or vibration. And this access to communication is further extended by various mobile applications that allow typing in  Morse code messages, which one can carry out with the help of taps or vibrations on the device. Therefore, this flexibility of Morse code provides an important means to help achieve this communication in different situations.

## C.  Comparison of Existing Morse Code Applications

Many other Morse code applications,  that  bring  it  to the  disabled  for  communication  purposes,  fail  to  provide requisite  accessibility  features  that  are  essential  for  dual disabilities.  For  instance,  The  Morse-Comm app by Sachdeva et al. is an example of a communication application using Morse code with input by gestures. While this may seem novel, the highly dependent nature of an exact recognition of the intended gestures by the interface makes it pretty hard and  time-consuming  for  a  user  with  motor  impairments, resulting in miscommunication at times [14].

Similarly, Gboard (Google Keyboard) allows Morse code entry,  but  taking  precise  time,  which  is  very challenging  for  users  with  fine  motor  control  problems: mistakes will most likely happen frequently and can make communication be misunderstood. The application at best has badly adjusted timings, further decreasing the chances of being accessed [15].

While the Samsung Good Vibes app enables communication for individuals with both visual and auditory  deficiencies  by  converting  Morse  code  into  text, it  lacks  dynamic  haptic  feedback,  an  essential  component for  any  user  relying  on  this  form  of  real-time  navigation and  support  for  communication.  This  may  weaken  its effectiveness  for  individuals  relying  heavily  on  touch  in navigating themselves through the environment [16].

The application presented in the paper overcomes these deficiencies  by  incorporating  various  features  explicitly designed for dual-disabled users. Continuous haptic feedback, in this way, allows users to feel every input, hence helping  to  increase  accuracy  and  decrease  dependence  on precise motor skills or visual input. Customizable sensitivity for  input  addition  and  real-time  timing  feedback  further smoothen  Morse  code  entry,  making  it  more  adaptable. This  application  goes  beyond  sheer  communication  to  an unparalleled edge in the field of education. By incorporating real-time word suggestions, it is much easier for the users to input Morse, hence it is a platform for inclusive learning. Further,  it  enhances  both  the  independent  learning  and structured interaction with teachers, making this application uniquely  suited  for  educational  applications  within  dualdisabled individuals.

## III. Proposed Solution: Enhanced Morse Code Application for Dual-Disabled Students

Building  upon  the  limitations  identified  in  existing technologies and applications, our proposed solution introduces  a  more  inclusive  Morse  code  application  that specifically  aids  users  who  have  both  visual  and  auditory impairments. This will not only provide a communication avenue  for  dual-disabled  persons  but  will  also  enhance learning  by  incorporating  ease  of  use,  accessibility,  and effective learning. Here,  our  design  incorporates  realtime  feedback,  adaptive  input  customization,  and  word suggestions  in  NLP-driven  Morse  code  to  make  it  more intuitive and responsive to individual user needs than any other application currently available.

The next sections describe the main distinctive features that are the reason for our solution, focusing on innovations in tactile feedback, educational tools, and enhanced accuracy in Morse code entry.

## A.  Morse Typing Window

The home screen of the application, depicted in Figure 1.a, where buttons to all available features link directly to the respective feature window. Besides navigation through buttons, it also offers Morse code-based input for navigating through the application. Further, users can navigate through this  application  by  voice  commands,  making  it  more versatile and friendly for the user.



Fig. 1: a. Home Screen (left most), b. Morse Typing Window (Middle),  c. Text-to-Morse Window (Right Most)





The Morse Typing Window, shown in Figure 1.b, is designed to facilitate Morse code input through a split-screen interface, whereby the top portion denotes 'dots' (.) and the bottom portion denotes 'dashes' (\_). The working process of  this  window  is  mentioned  in  Figure  2.  It  initiates  its

Fig. 2: The Working Process of Morse Typing Window



Copyright © IEEE-2025 ISBN: 979-8-3315-2814-0

processing by checking if the volume button is pressed, and upon pressing it, voice prompts guide the user through the Morse Typing Window. If the volume button is not pressed, it  waits for user input. Later, the application differentiates short  presses  from  long  ones  in  order  to  detect  dots  and dashes. If the top portion is pressed, it is interpreted as a 'dot'. When the top portion is long pressed, it deletes the last morse character typed. Similarly, If the bottom portion is  pressed,  it  is  interpreted  as  a  'dash'.  When  the  bottom portion is long pressed, it enters a whitespace character. This system not only allows correct input but also facilitates realtime correction by allowing deletion and spacing whenever needed. After catching each one of the Morse inputs, dot or dash, the application translates it into the corresponding character and then keeps listening to other inputs. If, after translation,  the  volume  button  is  clicked  again,  it  speaks out the word typed out so far, thus allowing users to crosscheck their progress. This continues until the user chooses to  finish,  at  which  time  the  session  of  the  Morse  Typing Window closes.

## B.  Text-to-Morse

The  functionality of Text-to-Morse, mentioned  in Figure 1.c, is one of the most essential in ensuring increased communication for visually and hearing-impaired students. The  application  provides  an  easy  interface  for  a  user  to enter texts, which then get converted into Morse code. Such conversion  will  definitely  ensure  better  communication among  peers  and  between  educators  and  students,  thus promoting a friendly educational environment. To further support  understanding,  the  Morse  code  is  vibrated  to  the students,  hence  creating  an  avenue  where  they  can  feel

The flow of the Text-to-Morse process, as in  Figure 3, beings by checking if the volume button has been pressed for  voice  instructions.  If  that  is  not  the  case,  it  waits  for text  entry. ## C.  Speech-to-Morse

Another functionality in this application is Speech-toMorse, which will add to the realms of accessibility, making communication easier. This interface, shown in Figure 4.a, includes  a  'Speak'  button,  which,  upon  being  clicked, activates the microphone that picks up words spoken across it as shown in Figure 4.b. Detected speech is converted to text and then translated into Morse code by the application. It also allows the user to make the learning more interactive by pushing the 'Read Morse Code' button and providing two vibrations representing the Morse code sequence of the spoken text. In this way, students with visual and auditory impairments will feel the speech, which shall increase the understanding and engagement in their minds.

Besides  that,  there  is  a  'Save'  button,  which  saves the  detected  speech  in  PDF  format  for  future  reference. Therefore, this will be one of the most taken-intoconsideration  benefits  in  educational  settings  because  it allows  teachers  to  instruct  or  explain  something  by  only speaking, while the application translates it and informs the student both with tactile vibrations and saved records.

## D.  Morse for PDF

The Morse for PDF feature is designed to allow students who have both vision and hearing impairments to translate lecture materials into Morse code for learning.

When this window is opened, the user is prompted to open a PDF document to be translated into Morse code, as in Figure 5.a and Figure 5.b. Once the PDF is opened, the user will click 'Vibrate Morse Code' which will provide them the  content  as  Morse  code  vibrations.  This  feature  would provide the users with the capability of interacting with their study materials through tactile input, hence making learning more accessible and independent.

## E.  Tutorial Window

The Tutorial window, as shown in Figure 7, represents an  interactive  Morse  learning  experience  with  both  voice and Morse instructions that take users interactively through a fun game. This allows the generation of prompts to type specific  characters  or  numbers  in  Morse.  This  increases their acquaintance with Morse input. In this way, it is fun and easy to teach the Morse code in practice to a student targeting  effective  practice.  This  integration  of  audio  and tactile instructions ensures that indeed users can also follow through, therefore being an effective tool in education for dual-disabled students.

## F.  Article Reader

Article  Reader  aggregates  articles  from  the  internet into one place and gives a list available to be read by the user  (Figure  8.a).  When  an  article  selected,  it  displays  in both text and Morse versions to let users choose how they would  like  to  engage  with  the  content  as  seen  in  Figure 8.b.  Clicking  the  Read  button  reads  the  article  aloud  for accessibility by visually impaired users. The selection of the Vibrate button makes the article vibrate in Morse code if clicked on, allowing dual-disabled users to feel an article by touch. Accessibility of information therefore improves, being  enhanced  through  such  adaptations  of  content  to diverse accessibility needs.

All of the features listed above together strive to reach a  point  where  dual-disabled students can comfortably use and access their education. Besides its pedagogical value, the application is also used as an effective communication tool  because  through  the  software,  dual-disabled  students who cannot write or use their vocal cords can communicate efficiently with other students and educators in Morse code.

## Bangla Speech-To-Braille Interaction Device for Visual and Hearing Impaired



Rafia Rizwana Rahim, Atik Ishrak Al Nahian, Rakiba Benta Kalam, Atanu Sen Gupta, and Nusrat Binta Nizam

Abstract This research project proposes a prototype design for a refreshable braille display to convert Bangla Speech to Braille output. It aims to be a communication and learning module for deaf-blind or blind people to pursue inclusive education. First, we took speech input from a transducer, and speech recognition was done by DeepSpeech, a Natural Language Processing framework developed by Mozilla. Then, the converted text output was linked with its corresponding braille pins and wasshowninarefreshable braille display. Finally, the entire computation was carried away by a Raspberry Pi. We have proposed the usage of our prototype as a special ICT device in the journey towards inclusive education in Bangladesh. In addition, future development has been suggested to broaden the periphery of the project.

Keywords DeepSpeech · Bangla natural language processing · Refreshable Braille display

## 1 Introduction

The goal of this research project is to create a refreshable braille display that accepts Bangla speech as input and outputs active braille pins. It is primarily intended to serve as a communication and learning tool for deaf-blind persons, particularly those aged 5-18. When we saw that there were few technologies for blind students and none for deaf-blind pupils, we were inspired to create such a product. There are 285 million visually impaired (VI) people in the world [1], where 90% of them are from developing countries [1]. Nearly 19 million children below 15 age group are VI [1].

## 2 Literature Review

Even while Braille Displays are pretty famous, adding a language-specific speech to the braille module is the real issue. It is a pity that Bangla, the world's seventh most spoken language, lacks a fully developed speech-to-text module that we may use in our project. We found the closest potential work that can assist us in creating our voice recognition module when searching for a good speech recognition approach. We found the following suggested approaches and theoretical results for Bangla Speech to Text Conversion Linear Predictive Coding (LPC) and Cepstral Coefficients for feature extraction and a Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) approach for recognition [9], Mel Frequency Cepstrum [10], Unsuper vised learning by recognizing repeated patterns [11], tokenizing the input speech [12], unsupervised term discovery to cluster repeated patterns [13], classification of unlabeled data [14]. More recent studies used CNN, RNN, and LSTM-based models for Bangla speech recognition purpose [17]. Also, augmented dataset has given closer mnemonics and better results than other studies [18]. Hannun et al. [15] showed a deep recurrent neural network architecture to recognize continuous speech. Experimentation showed that it gave highly accurate Transcriptions to continuous speech. With data of 600hours, the model yields 90% accuracy for Mandarin Chinese and English. They published as Mozilla's opensource framework DeepSpeech, which can be used to model any language for speech recognition and, therefore, has been used in our research. To support the requirements of DeepSpeech, we searched for speech to text dataset and found that the dataset provided by the openslr platform is the most suitable for our purpose [19]. Previous studies have also been done using the same dataset. For Example Sultana et al. (2012) used the corresponding dataset in their SAPI-based speech recognition module [16]. Therefore, this dataset is a proven one to be used in our research.

## 3 Dataset Preparation

A huge preprocessed dataset is the primary need of any deep learning frame work. DeepSpeech also requires over 100,000 audio and text samples and numerous other auxiliary files to begin its training. The steps of dataset preprocessing can be described sequentially.

## 3.1 Dataset Selection and Download

We looked for an open-source Bangla audio dataset and found 250 thousand audio of 347 h and corresponding text data in the openslr platform [19], where it contained 229 h of unique speech to text data and the rest of the dataset was either repeated or augmented. The dataset was in a very raw format and needed preprocessing.

## 3.2 .wav Conversion, Riff Code Addition and Audio Data Debug

DeepSpeech only accepts.wav audio files with a specified riff code attached before the audio file. Using AudioConverter 0.3.0 [22], we converted the .flac files to.wav files with riff code attached. Then we built ftwdebug.py [21], a debugger program to see if any audio files were missing the riff code. Furthermore, DeepSpeech will not accept audio input with a duration of fewer than 3 s. As a result, we built a new debugger code [21] to erase and exclude all audio with a duration of fewer than 3 s.

## 3.3 Corresponding Text Manual Correction and Unicode Debug

DeepSpeech requires audio files to be accompanied by a text file that contains no numerical characters or punctuation marks. As a result, all of these had to be manually erased. The openslr dataset included a .tsv file containing file names (without extensions), anonymous ids, and matching text for each audio file. As a result, we initially copied it into Excel. Next, we divided the spreadsheet file into 250 separate excel files, each with 1000 items, to make human corrections easier. All punctuation marks and emoji were then manually removed. In this case, the number of characters was replaced with Bangla text. Unfortunately, the text contained an unexplained error, and DeepSpeech could not locate Unicode for the Bangla script. So we built xldebug.py [21], a debugging program to see if each of our characters has a UTF+8 code associated with it.

## 3.4 .csv File Generation

DeepSpeechrequires the following format for audio text in.csv: filename (with extension), file size (in Kilobytes), text. To create such a .csv file, we created the file name size.py script [21], which can create .csv files containing the file name and size. Our audio files were divided into 14 folders, each with 14 subfolders. We put all audio files in one folder and made a .csv file with the file name and size.

## 3.5 Text Input In .csv Files

Now, we had two files one having the file name and corresponding texts, the other having file name and size. We needed to merge these two excel sheets concerning file names. When we did so, we saw that several audio files did not have written text with them. Also, some of the audio files did not exist in our dataset, but their file name and text were written in the excel file. So we needed to delete those entries manually. Here is our sequential operation we copied the file names in a text file for each segmented excel file. Then we run a code File Copy Reading txt to Folder and Check.py [21] that will copy the file reading the .txt from our audio folder. It will also show which files do not exist. So we can remove those entries manually. This same process had to be repeated 250 times. Then we can merge text with file name and size .csv file.

## 3.6 Training, Development, and Testing Dataset

DeepSpeech requires three folders having audio files and .csv files containing the file name, size, and text. They are called training (train), development (dev), test folders. In each training cycle, train, dev, and test contain 70, 20, and 10% audio data. For example, we trained our model 5 times, each with 40-80 thousand data. For this, we created test, train, dev folders for each training using the above-stated manner.

## 3.7 Generating alphabet.txt File

We needed a code to generate all alphabet of Bangla in a text file. Moreover, we needed it to tell the training framework that we are using a different alphabet set than English, and it is Unicode based. So, we created a python code alphabet.py [21] that generates an alphabet of Bengali language and saves it in a text file.

## 3.8 Generating a Scorer File

A scorer is a language model, and DeepSpeech uses it to improve the accuracy of the transcription. A language model predicts which words are more likely to come after one another. The scorer determines the likelihood of words appearing together. DeepSpeech's default scorer is trained using the LibriSpeech dataset. The LibriSpeech dataset is based on LibriVox, an open repository of public domain and out-of-copyright works. Therefore, we needed scorer training DeepSpeech in another language, training a speech recognition model for a particular domain. In our case, it is day-to-day formal Bangla speeches mostly available in news reports and to improve the accuracy of the transcription.

DeepSpeech supports the optional use of an external scorer. We collected many of the sentences from Bangla Wikipedia through a module named banglaWIKI corpus, which allowed us to scrap through Wikipedia and download the Bengali sentences, which we will use to build the scorer. It supports our DeepSpeech model with predefined sentences that we regularly use. If a model does not understand a voice fully, it tries to match its word with the scorer file and shows outputs accordingly.

## 4.2 Bangla Text to Braille (TTB) Conversion

Every character of the Bangla language has a unique Unicode. For every Unicode, a character has a unique combination of braille pins. Braille is a language where letters are shown via the combination of 6 pins in a braille cell numbered vertically. Most of the letters in Bangla need one set of braille pins to resemble the character. Nevertheless, prefixes and suffixes are incorporated when it comes down to words, joint letters, or sentences. We have followed all the rules of Bangla Braille to Convert Bangla Text to Braille pins. The following rules were considered while setting conditional loops in the TTB code, btb.py. Our code (btb.py) [21] first splits every word in the text obtained from DeepSpeech. If there is any joint letter, it is detected by Unicode of hosonto between those letters. Then the braille pins are detected according to the rules of Bangla Braille Adaption [27]. The numbers of braille pins are the input of our refreshable braille display. The display is designed so that the output is shown cell by cell, one cell at an instant (Figs. 2, 3 and 4).

## 4.3 Refreshable Braille Display

Electrical Circuit Design : We have used six independent servo motors with a linear actuation system to show the braille pins obtained. Here the servo has been controlled by a Pulse Width Modulation (PWM) controller named Adafruit PCA9685. This PWM controller takes input from Raspberry Pi and sends corresponding signals to the servos. The servos' precise motion helps to step up the braille pin when necessary. We have done our circuit design using Fritzing [24]. Once our circuit was complete, we combined all of our programs into a single file called Speech to Braille Hardware.py. [21] While operating, this program requests the user's input audio stream, transforms it to text, and then generates the matching Braille pin numbers and servos. The electrical simulation was also used to create a printed circuit board (PCB).

Mechanical Part Design : We intend to use a rack and pinion gear system as our primary hardware and designed the device accordingly in SolidWorks [25]. However, because of the lockout in the Covid-19 issue, we were unable to 3D print our rack and pinions. As a result, we devised a less expensive alternative: the reverse crank mechanism. The servo shaft will be attached to a crank here. The connecting rod will be linked to the other end of the crank. The connecting rod is connected to a piston (braille pin) that can reciprocate with a predetermined slot. As a result, the reverse crank mechanism acts as a linear actuator, converting the servo's rotating motion to the linear motion of the braille pin [26].

After trailing for several weeks, we saw that our prototype was working fine. So we did our final assembly. Here the circuit is mounted in a 10cm*10cm*10cm box. The microphone adapter is directed outward. The raspberry pi is getting power from a power bank.

## 6 Discussion

The ASR results described in the figures are self-explanatory to understand how our ASRmodelisdealing with test data. The model can successfully identify mnemonics of common verbs, vowels, consonant, and vowel modifiers. More data can reduce the loss in this model and therefore give a better accuracy. We have realized that perfecting any speech recognition module could take months. Furthermore, it is a one-way communication medium; a person may convert speech to braille but not vice versa using the same instruments. How- ever, we do not consider all of this a weakness; instead, we believe that it will only open the doors to a plethora of opportunities soon. It is self-evident that the accuracy of any ASR model improves as the dataset size grows. We used a dataset that was created by chopping up news snippets for our project. As a result, it is unable to distinguish the dialect of a specific Bangladeshi district. Its vocabulary has to be expanded in order to recognize more complex works. As a result, the production of datasets is our key priority as we upgrade our prototype. Our prototype is a one-way communication medium. We intend to incorporate Braille to Speech using the same devices. So when a deaf-blind person is asked something using our device, he/she can get an immediate response. Such de- vices can work as their eyes and voice. This complete package can help millions of people to demolish the barriers around them and add them to mainstream education.

Our equipment is simple to use for school-aged youngsters who are deaf-blind. Because it records audio and subsequently translates it to braille, the gadget can be used as a note-taking device for them and a great aid while listening to lessons. As a result, implementing our initiative following the Bangladesh Education Ministry's objective can illuminate the path to inclusive education on its own.

## IV. EVALUATION METHODOLOGY

The AI-driven assistive learning system is developed through  five  key  steps  to  ensure  accessibility,  efficiency, and  engagement.  First,  diverse  datasets  from  VIPs,  SIPs, and  multilingual  users  are  collected  and  pre-processed  for accurate model  training. Next, advanced  deep  learning models like Transformers, CNNs, and GANs are trained to support language, speech, and sign interpretation with supervised and reinforcement learning. Then, the AI models are deployed on cloud platforms featuring adaptive UI and secure  communication.  Extensive  user  testing  refines  the system  based  on  feedback.  Finally,  continuous  monitoring and  updates  maintain  performance,  scalability,  and  data security.

To validate the effectiveness, scalability, and inclusivity of the proposed AI-driven Multiagent Fused Framework (IHY), a comprehensive evaluation methodology was employed, combining both quantitative and qualitative approaches. Testing  was  carried  out  across  three environments -Static  Mode,  Dynamic  Mode,  and  Mixed Mode -to simulate varied digital learning contexts. Comparative  benchmarking  was  also  performed  against existing assistive learning models, demonstrating the superiority of IHY in accessibility and adaptability. Finally, the system underwent rigorous security and reliability testing  through  stress  scenarios  and  anomaly  detection  to ensure robustness and data integrity.

## VI. CONCLUSION

This study presents a comprehensive and innovative solution  to  a  longstanding  challenge  in  digital  education ensuring equitable  access  for  visually  and  speech-impaired learners.  The  proposed  'I  HELP  YOU'  (IHY)  framework, built on an AI-driven Multi-Agent Fused Framework (MAFF), successfully integrates advanced technologies such as Conversational User Interfaces (CUIs), multimodal transcoding,  haptic  feedback,  adaptive  feedback  loops,  and multilingual  linguistic  tools.  Through  a  carefully  designed system architecture, the IHY  framework  addresses  the limitations  of  existing  assistive  tools  that  often  function  in isolation and  lack  interoperability.  Empirical  evaluation involving 120 diverse participants demonstrated the effectiveness  of  the  system  across  multiple  dimensions, including accessibility, accuracy, user engagement, personalization,  and  security.  The  system  achieved  high accuracy  rates in speech-to-text (92.4%),  text-to-speech (95.7%),  and  sign  language  recognition  (88.2%),  while maintaining real-time responsiveness. It also recorded significant improvements in learning retention, tutor-learner interaction, multilingual support, and digital literacy development. These outcomes validate the practical feasibility  and  impact  of  IHY  as  a  transformative  tool  for inclusive  education.  Moreover,  the  IHY  framework  proves to  be  scalable  and  adaptable,  supporting  both  static  and dynamic modes of instruction and enabling real-time, context-aware content delivery. Its cloud-based deployment and cross-device compatibility extend its reach across geographies  and  learning  environments,  promoting  global accessibility. The incorporation of robust security protocols, including  end-to-end  encryption  and  anomaly  detection, ensures a safe and trustworthy platform for sensitive learner data.  In  essence,  this  research  redefines  the  paradigm  of assistive digital learning by moving from isolated tools to an integrated, intelligent, and learner-centric system. The IHY framework  not  only  bridges  the  digital  divide  for  learners with disabilities but also sets a foundation for future advancements in inclusive education through artificial intelligence. Future work may focus on longitudinal studies, integration  with  national  digital  learning  platforms,  and expansion  into  additional  disability  categories  to  further generalize and enhance the model's applicability.

## AdvAncements In Iot And edge computIng for dIsAbled IndIvIduAls

The incorporation of IoT and edge computing technologies into assistive systems for disabled individuals represents a major leap forward in enhancing their autonomy, and hence their quality of life. By processing data locally at the edge, these systems significantly reduce latency, bolster security, and offer real-time responsiveness, an essential component in assistive technologies.

Real-Time Processing and Secure Communication: Edge computing enables real-time processing, which is a cornerstone of modern assistive technologies.  For  example,  in  communication assistance for people with severe disabilities, such as those who are paralyzed, the ability to interpret and transmit neural signals quickly and accurately is paramount.

Rajesh et  al. [12]  demonstrated  that  edge computing can facilitate secure brain-brain communication by processing the electroencephalography signals (EEG) at the edge, thus ensuring that sensitive data are transmitted securely and promptly between the patient and the caregiver. This approach enhances the efficiency of these assistive systems and ensures that critical communication occurs without delays, which could compromise the user's safety and well-being.

Security is another crucial advantage provided by edge computing. By handling data encryption  and  decryption  locally,  edge  computing minimizes the risks of transmitting sensitive information over potentially insecure networks. This is particularly important for assistive devices, as these devices often handle personal and sensitive data. Rajesh et al. [12] highlighted how their proposed Novel Tiny Symmetric Algorithm (NTSA) efficiently encrypts data at the edge, ensuring secure communication in IoT-enabled assistive systems.

Accessibility  and  Quality  of  Life  Enhancement: Edge computing also plays a pivotal role in improving accessibility and ensuring a higher quality of life for disabled individuals. Rathee et al. [13] emphasized the importance of designing edge computing systems that prioritize accessibil- ity and are designed to meet the unique needs of disabled users. Their research focused on creating trusted edge computing frameworks that not only enhance the functionality of assistive devices but also ensure that these systems are user friendly and accessible to all, regardless of the user's specific impairments.

By reducing dependency on centralized cloud services, edge computing allows for more personalized and adaptive responses from assistive technologies. For example, wearable devices that assist visually impaired people can take advantage of edge computing to process real-time video data, providing immediate and contextually relevant feedback. This enhances the user's situational awareness and autonomy, allowing them to navigate their environment more effectively.

Energy Efficiency and Portability: Energy efficiency is another critical factor in deploying IoT and edge computing in assistive technologies. These devices, often portable and used in various environments, require efficient energy management to function effectively over extended periods. By processing data locally, Edge computing frameworks reduce the need for constant communication with cloud servers, thereby conserving energy and extending battery life.

Rathee et  al. [13]  also  discussed  how  edge computing can be optimized for low-power devices, making these technologies more viable for daily use by disabled individuals. This focus on energy efficiency enhances the usability of these devices and ensures that they remain operational in situations where access to power sources is limited.

Advances  in  IoT  and  edge  computing  are transforming assistive technologies for disabled individuals, making them more secure, responsive, and energy efficient. By enabling real-time processing, improving data security, and improving energy management, these technologies significantly improve the quality of life for disabled users. As research in this field progresses, it will be crucial to optimize these systems further to meet the specific needs of disabled individuals, ensuring that the full potential of IoT and edge computing is realized in creating more inclusive and accessible environments.

## dIscussIon And future worK

The integration of IoT and edge computing has shown  tremendous  potential  in  transforming assistive technologies for people with disabilities, particularly those with hearing and vision impairments. Our research demonstrates that leveraging an edge-IoT-based framework incorporating MLLMs, multi-object tracking, and scene understanding can significantly enhance accessibility and quality of life for this population. The bottleneck computation highlights the need for further optimization, in architecture, resources or algorithmic choices, as real-time applications demand more effi cient processing. Addressing this issue requires a combination of more powerful hardware, refined algorithms, and software optimized for edge computing environments.

Energy efficiency remains a challenge, particularly  given  the  computational  demands  of AI  models  integrated  into  portable  devices. Although edge computing reduces reliance on cloud communication, the energy required to run these models can be substantial, 250Wh on AWS against 15Wh on Jetson Nano for the Whisper small model, for example. Future efforts should focus on refi  ning these models to operate eff  ectively within the constraints of edge devices, by developing  lightweight  versions  that  maintain functionality while consuming less energy.

Despite these hurdles, the potential benefi  ts of this technology are substantial. Real-time transcription for impaired hearing people and scene descriptions  for  the  visually  impaired  people off  er signifi  cant advances in accessibility, particularly in educational and entertainment contexts. In addition, the framework we developed could be extended to support other types of impairments, providing new tools for navigation, learning, and more.

Future research should focus on addressing these limitations by developing more efficient algorithms, enhancing hardware capabilities, and further optimizing the system for real-time applications. It will also be crucial to expand use cases to cover a broader spectrum of disabilities and to adapt technology to diverse environments, particularly those with limited resources, to ensure widespread adoption.

- Transparency and Explainable: It is essential for AI/ML algorithms to be transparent and explainable, enabling users to comprehend decision-making processes and providing avenues for recourse in case of inaccuracies. Transparency fosters trust and accountability within the e-learning environment.
- Accountability and Oversight: Mechanisms should be in place to ensure that developers and stakeholders are responsible for the ethical consequences of AI/ML-enabled e-learning platforms. Oversight mechanisms, ethical review boards, and regulatory frameworks can aid in upholding compliance with ethical standards and directives.
- Bias Mitigation: In the realm of AI/ML, it has been recognized that algorithms have the potential to inadvertently perpetuate biases that exist within the data used for their training. To address this issue, developers are advised to take proactive measures to mitigate bias by utilizing a wide range of diverse datasets, incorporating fairnessaware algorithms into their models, and conducting regular audits and monitoring activities to detect and rectify bias in the decision-making processes of AI systems.
- Informed Consent: A fundamental principle in the ethical use of AI/ML technologies is the concept of informed consent, which asserts that users should have the autonomy to make knowledgeable decisions regarding the collection and utilization of their data for AI/ML applications. This principle should be upheld by e-learning platforms, which are encouraged to provide transparent and easily understandable information about how user data is utilized, and to seek explicit consent from users prior to gathering or processing their data.
